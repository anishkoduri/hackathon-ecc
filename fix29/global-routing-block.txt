**GLOBAL-ROUTING — the factory routing is the automatic routing for ALL making/building work (fix #29, GLOBAL-ROUTING_F29).** Every prompt is answered by the orchestrator model; drones never answer the user directly and never decide scope (ORCHESTRATOR-ONLY_F29, extends ALWAYSLEAD_F27). The orchestrator keeps a queue of independent single-task jobs and dispatches drones into free slots — one drone per task, retired on completion, the next queued task dispatched into the freed slot (FACTORY-DISPATCH_F29); the orchestrator picks the best drone model for the task (fix #30 assigns DeepSeek to reasoning-heavy work and Qwen to coding work) and the concurrency by decomposability, and the cap is what the harness supports; work is partitioned by file/module boundary so no two drones share files or mutable state. Every drone brief carries the exact goal, the files in scope and explicitly out of scope, constraints, acceptance criteria, verification steps, and what to return — an incomplete brief means the drone does not start (DIRECTOR-BRIEF_F29). A drone that hits a decision its brief leaves unspecified stops and returns the question to the orchestrator instead of guessing; it never invents scope, files, or acceptance criteria (NO-GUESSING_F29). The orchestrator verifies every drone output against the brief's acceptance criteria (plus browser-qa for visual work, audits for code); failed output goes back to a drone with a correction brief, and unverified drone output is never presented as the orchestrator's own (VERIFY-MERGE_F29). Fan-out is bounded by efficiency, not just speed: the orchestrator justifies N>3 in one line, and drones carry minimal context, only the files and facts their partition needs (COST-AWARE_F29). This routing is automatic for ALL making/building work in every skill, not opt-in — a skill that dispatches implementation work follows it, and a dispatch with no factory routing is off-skill improvisation and fails the audit. Live factory behavior (per-model dispatch, slot refill at scale) is harness-gated: in Cursor, per-subagent model selection is unreliable and in-IDE parallelism caps around 4 with no slot refill, so the factory lights up fully on the later port, not in Cursor — the rules are built as designed, not shrunk to fit Cursor.
