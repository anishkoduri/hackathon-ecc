# Hero review

You are reviewing the first screen of a page that is still being built. You did not build it and will not be told why anything is the way it is. Your inputs, and nothing else: the four `hero-*` screenshots, the stamp's first two lines, the Picks block's Material, Signature, and Hero-moment rows (the Hero-moment row, fix #23 HERO_F23, records the dealt amazing moment — moment + composition + motion — the hero was built to carry; judge the screen against it), and on a theme build the theme's picture. Batch your reads; stop reading by the tenth turn and write.

First line: `findings` or `no-findings`. The word is a craft status, not a ship/fix verdict — it rests on the craft questions below (headline, composition, visual, signature, type); template observations are candidate evidence for `browser-qa` and never drive it.

Then at most five findings, one line each, worst first. Ask:

- Does the headline say something only this product could say, in seven words or fewer? (Copy craft — whether the headline carries the product's own nouns and claim. A headline that would pass unchanged on a competitor's page is recorded as candidate evidence for `browser-qa`, not a finding of this review, and never drives the first word.)
- Is the content centred in the screen, or does it stop early over a band of nothing? Is the visual beside the text, a real picture (the user's, a photograph, the product built), and does it hold its half of the screen at 60% of its height or more? Where the shape puts no image in the fold (a Marquee, a Poster or a Banner hero), the type is the visual: does it fill the screen? At 1920 the container and the type hold their 1440 size by rule, so extra air there is not a finding.
- Does anything read as a template: a centred stack, a pill or a label above the headline, a gradient blob, an empty coloured box where a picture should be, a drawn illustration, a row of counting marks, two filled buttons side by side? A filled primary beside an outlined or text action that goes somewhere else is the control pair, and a sample of the product built in a slot is the product; neither is a finding. Grey bars or outline cards inside a slot are. **Append what reads as a template to `.hallmark/candidate-evidence.md` as candidate evidence for `browser-qa` (screen + what it reads as) — it is not a finding of this review and does not drive its first word: `browser-qa` alone executes the distinctiveness bar in the visual loop.**
- Is the page's signature, its strongest real thing, on this screen? Delete the picture in your head: if the screen still says the same thing, the picture is too weak or too small.
- Does the screen carry the dealt hero moment from the Picks block's Hero-moment row (fix #23 HERO_F23) — the moment, the distinctive composition, the motion — or is the hero merely adequate? A correct but unremarkable hero is recorded as candidate evidence for `browser-qa`, not a finding of this review, and never drives the first word: the amazing verdict belongs to `browser-qa` alone.
- Does the type carry the theme at this size?
- With the theme's picture: is this first screen as finished as its first screen, in a composition of its own?

On a custom run (`theme: custom`) the composition is free: skip the centred, beside-the-text and 60% questions, and ask instead whether this screen is one you would expect a model to make for this prompt. If it is, say what gives it away — as candidate evidence for `browser-qa`, not as a finding of this review: the distinctiveness verdict belongs to `browser-qa` alone.

On a redesign the page's content is fixed and what sits below the first screen is the old page, not yet rebuilt: neither is a finding. Never ask for copy, an action or a visual the page does not have; say in one line what the owner could supply.

What the theme file prescribes (its blooms, its control pair) or refuses is not a finding. Where the theme file and its picture disagree, the theme file wins over its picture. Do not fix anything.
